\section*{1.\ \ What I set out to do, and how I'd frame the result}
I tried to reproduce \textbf{Table III} of Sadka (2010, JFE) --- the time-series regressions of
11 equal-weighted hedge-fund style portfolios on the Fung--Hsieh factors and the Sadka liquidity
factor --- as a clean baseline we could later extend with our own factors. I want to be honest up
front: this is a \textbf{strong closest-feasible replication on the current WRDS/LSEG TASS vintage},
\emph{not} an exact reproduction of Sadka's 2009-era TASS sample. The sign pattern and the main
qualitative contrasts come through clearly; the magnitudes and counts differ, and I explain why.

\section*{2.\ \ Data and specification (kept faithful to the table)}
Monthly TASS returns, Jan 1994--Dec 2008; Live + Graveyard; monthly, net-of-fees, USD, excess of
the risk-free rate; 11 self-classified styles; equal-weighted monthly style portfolios; OLS
regressions on (M1) MKT-RF + Liquidity and (M2) the full Fung--Hsieh seven factors + Liquidity. I
deliberately kept Table IV's rolling-beta / decile rules \emph{out}.

\section*{3.\ \ Step by step: what I did, and what came out}
\textbf{(a) Liquidity factor.} \emph{Input:} the WRDS Sadka file (permanent-variable component),
used as-is. \emph{Output:} the crisis months are all negative, matching Figure 1 --- 1998-09
$=-0.016$, 2001-01 $=-0.012$, 2007-08 $=-0.012$, 2008-09 $=-0.031$. This passed, so I kept it as the
main factor.

\textbf{(b) Fung--Hsieh factors + Table II.} \emph{Input:} Ken French (MKT-RF, SMB), David Hsieh
(PTFS), FRED (GS10, BAA for $\Delta$TERM/$\Delta$CREDIT). \emph{Output:} all 28 pairwise
correlations land within 0.05 of the paper, with the right signs. The key liquidity row:

\textbf{(c) TASS sample.} \emph{Input:} I pulled the current LSEG schema (\texttt{tr\_tass}):

What I see: \textbf{all 11 liquidity signs match}. Strictly at 5\% ($|t|\ge1.96$) in Model 2,
six styles are clearly positive and significant (Convertible Arbitrage, Emerging Markets, Equity
Market Neutral, Event Driven, Long/Short Equity, Multi-Strategy); Fund of Funds sits right at the
cutoff; Fixed Income Arbitrage is positive but short of significance; Global Macro and Managed
Futures are weak --- as in the paper. \textbf{Dedicated Short Bias is negative and insignificant},
and its market beta is $-0.88$ (Sadka $-0.97$), a check I leaned on. So the qualitative result
reproduces; I'd flag that several \emph{magnitudes} (Multi-Strategy, Long/Short, Fund of Funds)
come out smaller than Sadka's.

\section*{5.\ \ Sensitivity and the honest caveats}
\textbf{Data-error screen.} The raw current-vintage data contain a few impossible records (a
$+31{,}208\%$ monthly return, exact $99.0$ sentinels). I report three screens and make the
conservative one (S01) the main version. S01 removes only 8 observations and does \emph{not} delete
returns of the same order of magnitude as Sadka's published extremes (my post-screen max is 18.16
vs his 13.245):

\textbf{Sample counts.} My Overall N is 8{,}345 vs Sadka's 12{,}929. The gap is mostly the USD
filter: with all currencies I get 13{,}521 ($\approx$ his total), but I keep USD-only because the
paper specifies USD returns. \textbf{Managed Futures} is the weakest spot --- only $\sim$17 funds
carry that primary category in this vintage (Sadka had 964), and fund names are anonymized, so I
\emph{deliberately do not} force-map ``Other''/``Undefined'' funds into it. A few style counts vs
Sadka: Fund of Funds 2{,}458/4{,}268; Long/Short 2{,}509/3{,}574; Multi-Strategy 631/1{,}177.

\section*{6.\ \ My read}
I'd describe this as a strong, auditable, closest-feasible replication. The liquidity-risk pattern
that Sadka's Table III is about is clearly there on a completely different data vintage, which to
me is the reassuring part. I would not call it an exact match --- the magnitudes are softer and the
sample is smaller --- but every qualitative red line holds, and the code is set up so we can drop
additional factors straight into the same style-level regressions. Full numbers, all three screens,
and the audit trail are in the accompanying workbook.
