\section*{1.\ \ Objective and framing}
I set out to replicate Table III of Sadka (2010, JFE) --- the time-series regressions of 11
equal-weighted hedge-fund style portfolios on the Fung--Hsieh factors and the Sadka liquidity
factor --- using the current WRDS/LSEG TASS vintage, with the aim of building a clean style-level
baseline that could later be extended with additional factors. I made several attempts under
different reasonable data choices; the numbers reported here are from the specification that came
closest to the paper. I would not describe this as an exact reproduction of Sadka's original
2009-era TASS sample. The main qualitative pattern is close, but the sample counts and several
coefficient magnitudes differ, and I have tried to be explicit about why.

\section*{2.\ \ Data and specification}
Monthly TASS returns, Jan 1994--Dec 2008; Live + Graveyard; monthly, net-of-fees, USD, excess of
the risk-free rate; 11 self-classified styles; equal-weighted monthly style portfolios; OLS
regressions on (M1) MKT-RF + Liquidity and (M2) the full Fung--Hsieh seven factors + Liquidity.
The rolling-beta / decile rules of Table IV were deliberately kept out.

\section*{3.\ \ Step-by-step: inputs and outputs}
\textbf{(a) Liquidity factor.} \emph{Input:} the WRDS Sadka file (permanent-variable component),
used as-is. \emph{Output:} the crisis months are all negative, in line with Figure 1 --- 1998-09
$=-0.016$, 2001-01 $=-0.012$, 2007-08 $=-0.012$, 2008-09 $=-0.031$.

(PTFS), FRED (GS10, BAA for $\Delta$TERM/$\Delta$CREDIT). \emph{Output:} the 28 pairwise
correlations all fall within 0.05 of the paper, with matching signs. The key liquidity row:

\textbf{(c) TASS sample.} \emph{Input:} the current LSEG schema (\texttt{tr\_tass}):

\textbf{Interpretation.} All 11 Model-2 liquidity-loading signs match the paper. Strictly using
$|t|\ge1.96$ in Model 2, six styles are clearly positive and significant (Convertible Arbitrage,
Emerging Markets, Equity Market Neutral, Event Driven, Long/Short Equity, Multi-Strategy); Fund of
Funds is very close to the cutoff, Fixed Income Arbitrage is positive but not significant, and
Global Macro and Managed Futures remain weak --- consistent with the paper. Dedicated Short Bias
has a negative and insignificant liquidity loading and a market beta of $-0.88$ (Sadka $-0.97$).
Several magnitudes (Multi-Strategy, Long/Short Equity, Fund of Funds) come out smaller than
Sadka's.

\section*{5.\ \ Sensitivity and caveats}
\textbf{Data-error screen.} The raw current-vintage data contain a few extreme apparent
data-error records (a $+31{,}208\%$ monthly return; exact $99.0$ sentinels). I report three
screens; the conservative one (S01) is the main version. It removes only 8 observations and does
not delete returns of the same order of magnitude as Sadka's published extreme values (post-screen
maximum 18.16, compared with his published 13.245):

\textbf{Sample counts.} The Overall N is 8{,}345, versus Sadka's 12{,}929. The gap is largely the
USD filter: with all currencies the count is 13{,}521 ($\approx$ his total), but I keep USD-only
because the paper specifies USD returns. \textbf{Managed Futures} is the largest coverage caveat
--- only about 17 funds carry that primary category in this vintage (Sadka had 964), and fund
names are anonymized, so I do not force-map ``Other''/``Undefined'' funds into it. A few style
counts (replication vs Sadka): Fund of Funds 2{,}458/4{,}268; Long/Short 2{,}509/3{,}574;
Multi-Strategy 631/1{,}177.

\section*{6.\ \ Summary}
The main liquidity-risk pattern that Table III documents is present on this different data vintage,
even though it is not an exact match: the magnitudes are softer and the sample is smaller. The
qualitative contrasts hold, and the code is organized so that additional factors can be added to
the same style-level regressions. Full numbers, all three screens, and the audit trail are in the
accompanying workbook.
